15 A uniform quantum Airy transition for the regular family

This section proves a statement for an explicitly specified meromorphic Barnes integral on the one-parameter regular slice. It does not assert a transition theorem for arbitrary six angle parameters, a three-manifold invariant, or an identification with the cone-metric measure. No novelty claim is made.

15.1 The exact normalization and the result

The Airy function is the standard replacement for a Gaussian when two stationary points merge. In the normalization used here, Ai⁡(z)=12πi∫c−i∞c+i∞eY3/3−zY⁡dY,c>0,\operatorname{Ai}(z)=\frac1{2\pi i} \int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}e^{Y^3/3-zY}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dY, \qquad c>0, with the contour directed upward. 15.2 A uniform double-sine estimate from its real integral

The proof has three analytic tasks. The double-sine estimate first separates the exact quantum function into a smooth amplitude and a classical phase. A global contour estimate then proves that the portion far from the merging saddles is negligible. Finally a rescaling computes the Airy integral and its correction. The local Taylor expansion alone would accomplish only the third task; it would leave open whether another part of the contour dominates.

15.7 Interpretation and scope

The correction to the earlier pole argument is substantive. The exact quantum integrand is smooth at the correct fold; the remaining real-bb resonances are an explicit scalar factor outside the integral. For example, at η=ηE\eta=\eta_{\mathrm E} the scalar factor has candidate resonances along b2=3ηE−π2πm,m=1,2,….b^2=\frac{3\eta_{\mathrm E}-\pi}{2\pi m}, \qquad m=1,2,\ldots. The numerator in (15.3) is nonzero for sufficiently small bb, uniformly in each fixed physical window 0≤ζ≤Z0\le\zeta\le Z: the continuous positive function Ai⁡(ζ)\operatorname{Ai}(\zeta) has a positive minimum on [0,Z][0,Z], and the additive correction and error tend uniformly to zero. Thus zeros of the squared sine give genuine double poles of the original normalization in this window, including all sufficiently large mm in the displayed sequence. For negative ζ\zeta, the additive asymptotic remains uniform even at zeros of Ai\operatorname{Ai}; no uniform noncancellation or relative-error assertion is made there. The cleared quantity has the uniform asymptotic in the proposition even through these resonances.

In geometric terms, ζ=A23h2/3+O(A2h2/3),A≍b4/3.\zeta=\frac{A}{2\sqrt3\,h^{2/3}} +O\!\left(\frac{A^2}{h^{2/3}}\right), \qquad A\asymp b^{4/3}. The decaying Airy branch selected by the contour agrees with the decaying volume exponential: its classical saddle exponent is −2W/h=−W/(πb2)-2W/h=-W/(\pi b^2). The proposition is uniform for bounded ζ\zeta; it does not, by itself, justify taking ζ→∞\zeta\to\infty simultaneously with b→0b\to0 or keeping a nonzero tetrahedral volume fixed. Those matching limits require additional estimates.

This proves a quantum transition for one specified regular family, beyond the classical cubic calculation. Extension to arbitrary nondegenerate Euclidean tetrahedral shapes, comparison of normalizations used in a global state integral, and gluing into a topological invariant remain separate problems. In particular no new case of the colored-Jones volume conjecture, and no identification of the integrated quantum measure with the cone-metric measure, follows from this proposition alone.
