3 The ideal tetrahedron: an explicit volume integral

This is the first complete integral: a two-dimensional space of tetrahedral shapes becomes a triangle in two angle coordinates, and its density becomes constant. The number V0,4V_{0,4} computed here is the WP volume of that shape space, not the hyperbolic volume of a single tetrahedron. We keep the four vertex labels, so swapping vertices is not silently divided out.

3.1 Lambda lengths and the ambient cross-ratio

In the upper-half-space model, hyperbolic three-space is {(w,h):w∈ℂ,h>0}\{(w,h):w\in\mathbb C,\ h>0\} with metric (|⁡dw|2+⁡dh2)/h2(|\mathop{}\!\mathrm dw|^2+\mathop{}\!\mathrm dh^2)/h^2; its ideal boundary is ℂ∪{∞}\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}. A horosphere centered at a finite ideal point is a Euclidean sphere tangent to h=0h=0, while one centered at infinity is a horizontal plane. Its intersection with a face is a horocycle. A decoration chooses one such horocycle at each cusp. It supplies finite reference endpoints on otherwise infinitely long ideal edges; changing the decoration changes the lambda lengths but not the underlying surface.

Take the four labeled ideal vertices to be 0,1,∞,z0,1,\infty,z, where z=x+iy∈ℂ\{0,1}z=x+iy\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}. For y≠0y\ne0 their convex hull is a genuine ideal tetrahedron. The two half-planes describe its two orientation types.

For a decorated ideal triangulation, set λe=eδe/2\lambda_e=e^{\delta_e/2}, where δe\delta_e is the signed distance between the chosen horocycles along the edge. An overall fixed multiplier in the definition of λ\lambda does not change its logarithmic differential. With cyclically ordered edge lengths a,b,ca,b,c on each oriented triangle, Penner’s formula in convention (1.1) is ωWP=−2∑△(⁡dlog⁡a∧⁡dlog⁡b+⁡dlog⁡b∧⁡dlog⁡c+⁡dlog⁡c∧⁡dlog⁡a).\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}=-2\sum_{\triangle} \bigl(\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\log a\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\log b+ \mathop{}\!\mathrm d\log b\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\log c+ \mathop{}\!\mathrm d\log c\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\log a\bigr).(3.1) This is a form on decorated Teichmüller space that descends after forgetting the horocycles [85]; its magnitude agrees with the cuspidal limit of Mondello’s arc-coordinate formula [79].

The four decorations can be chosen so that λ0∞=λ1∞=λz∞=λ01=1,λ0z=r=|z|,λ1z=s=|1−z|.\lambda_{0\infty}=\lambda_{1\infty}=\lambda_{z\infty} =\lambda_{01}=1, \qquad \lambda_{0z}=r=|z|,\quad\lambda_{1z}=s=|1-z|.(3.2) For example, use equal Euclidean diameters for the three finite horospheres in the upper-half-space model and adjust their common scale together with the horosphere at infinity. The usual formula for the signed distance then gives (3.2). One may take diameter 11 for each finite horosphere and height 11 at infinity. The finite-edge formula is then λij=|zi−zj|\lambda_{ij}=|z_i-z_j|, and each edge to infinity has lambda length 11. Overlapping horospheres cause no problem: the distances are signed, and smaller embedded decorations can first be chosen and then rescaled.

Every face containing ∞\infty has at most one varying lambda length, so its contribution in (3.1) vanishes in this section of the decoration bundle. The remaining face has lengths 1,r,s1,r,s. Consequently the positive WP measure is ⁡dVWP=2|dlogr∧dlogs|=2|y||z|2|1−z|2⁡dx⁡dy.\boxed{ \mathop{}\!\mathrm dV_{\mathrm{WP}}=2\left|\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\log r\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\log s\right| =\frac{2|y|}{|z|^2|1-z|^2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dx\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dy.}(3.3) The Jacobian calculation is elementary: ⁡dlog⁡r=x⁡dx+y⁡dyr2,⁡dlog⁡s=(x−1)⁡dx+y⁡dys2.\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\log r=\frac{x\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dx+y\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dy}{r^2},\qquad \mathop{}\!\mathrm d\log s=\frac{(x-1)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dx+y\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dy}{s^2}. Their wedge is y⁡dx∧⁡dy/(r2s2)y\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dx\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dy/(r^2s^2).

At z=12+i32z=\tfrac12+i\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} the triangle (0,1,z)(0,1,z) is regular, so r=s=1r=s=1. The displayed density is then 3⁡dx⁡dy\sqrt3\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dx\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dy. This is a local density at a single shape; the next change of variables integrates over all shapes on one orientation sheet.

3.3 What happens at the punctures of the parameter plane?

Finite volume requires integrability, not pointwise decay of a coordinate density. Near z=0z=0, write z=ρeiϑz=\rho e^{i\vartheta}. Then 2|y||z|2|1−z|2=2|sin⁡ϑ|ρ(1+O(ρ)),⁡dx⁡dy=ρ⁡dρ⁡dϑ.\frac{2|y|}{|z|^2|1-z|^2} =\frac{2|\sin\vartheta|}{\rho}\,(1+O(\rho)), \qquad \mathop{}\!\mathrm dx\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dy=\rho\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\rho\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\vartheta. The singularity is integrable. The same is true at 11. At infinity the density is O(|z|−3)O(|z|^{-3}), again integrable against Euclidean area. Thus the requested decay is best formulated as a condition on the measure, not on its density without its coordinate area element.

Remark 3.1 (The coordinate is not conformal). Formula (3.3) is the pullback to the ambient ideal-vertex coordinate. It is not a formula for WP measure in the intrinsic conformal cross-ratio. In particular, its vanishing on the real locus does not mean that the WP form degenerates at an ordinary interior point of moduli space. The polyhedral parametrization is not a smooth coordinate across that locus. The Delaunay literature also distinguishes the embedding coordinate from uniformization [50, Section 3.3.1].

Nor do two ordinary closed angle triangles describe the full topology of the compactification without additional data. As y↓0y\downarrow0 with 0<x<10<x<1, two of the triangle angles tend to zero and the third to π\pi, independently of xx. A whole family of flat quadrilaterals approaches one angle-simplex vertex. Retaining that modulus requires a refinement of the boundary, such as a real blow-up. This has no effect on the integral over the open cells.
