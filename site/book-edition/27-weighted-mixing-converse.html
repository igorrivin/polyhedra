27 Filling-pair coefficients and a weighted mixing converse

The preceding converse criteria suggest retaining two independent sets of curve data. This section carries that proposal through three concrete steps. First, a tensor product of the coefficient construction recovers marked filling pairs, hence square-tiled differentials. Second, an exactly weighted count of Teichmüller orbit segments unfolds into a time integral of correlations. Third, an elementary de-averaging argument converts an assumed counting remainder into a mixing rate. An arithmetic torus calculation illustrates the additional cancellation needed to prove such a remainder. The identities and conditional implications below do not constitute a new proof of Teichmüller mixing.

27.1 Two independent character variables retain the pair

Keep a closed marked hyperbolic surface XX as an auxiliary device, and let ρ,σ\rho,\sigma be independent rank-two character variables. The length-valued product of Section 25 can be tensored with itself, retaining two separate length coordinates: 𝑬X(ρ)⊠𝑬X(σ)=∑C,DTC(ρ)TD(σ)(νCX⊠νDX).\mathbf E_X(\rho)\boxtimes\mathbf E_X(\sigma) =\sum_{C,D} T_C(\rho)T_D(\sigma)\, (\nu_C^X\boxtimes\nu_D^X). Here ⊠\boxtimes is the external product of locally finite discrete measures, and both CC and DD range over integral weighted multicurves. The two variables are essential: multiplication in one character algebra would resolve crossings between CC and DD, whereas their ordered pair is precisely the information required here.

Proposition 27.1 (The first corner of a pair coefficient). The coefficient of TC(ρ)TD(σ)T_C(\rho)T_D(\sigma) is supported in [ℓX(C),∞)×[ℓX(D),∞),[\ell_X(C),\infty)\times[\ell_X(D),\infty), and has an isolated atom of mass one at (ℓX(C),ℓX(D))(\ell_X(C),\ell_X(D)). Its remaining support misses this corner.

Proof. The one-variable unit-diagonal identity (25.10) gives νCX=δℓX(C)+τCX,supp⁡τCX⊂(ℓX(C),∞),\nu_C^X=\delta_{\ell_X(C)}+\tau_C^X, \qquad \operatorname{supp}\tau_C^X \subset(\ell_X(C),\infty), and similarly for DD. Expanding the external product proves the claim; local finiteness isolates the corner. Each finite rectangle of source lengths involves only finitely many source configurations, so the argument also applies before taking any transform. ◻

After this extraction, the marked labels retain i(C,D)i(C,D) and the topological filling condition. Thus one can restrict to filling pairs and apply weights to their associated flat differentials. The auxiliary lengths ℓX\ell_X provide the triangular extraction; the area of the resulting differential will be i(C,D)i(C,D), a different quantity. In particular, a bound on area is not a bound on both auxiliary hyperbolic lengths on the marked space. Passing to an area cutoff and a mapping-class quotient is an additional operation, whose quantitative cost is not supplied by fixed-coefficient convergence.

27.2 Filling pairs as actual coordinates for the flow

For definiteness let SS be closed, oriented, and of genus g≥2g\geq2. A pair (A,B)(A,B) of nonzero measured foliations is filling if i(A,C)+i(B,C)>0for every nonzero measured foliation C.i(A,C)+i(B,C)>0\qquad\text{for every nonzero measured foliation }C. The condition is stronger than i(A,B)>0i(A,B)>0. The realization theorem of Gardiner–Masur, also proved by Wentworth using harmonic maps, identifies filling pairs with marked nonzero holomorphic quadratic differentials [1, Theorem 1.6 and Corollary 1.7]; see also [2, Theorem 1.6] for the equivariant homeomorphism and its integral interpretation. Use the convention that A=𝒱(q)A=\mathcal V(q) is the vertical foliation with transverse measure |dx||dx|, and B=ℋ(q)B=\mathcal H(q) is the horizontal foliation with transverse measure |dy||dy|, in coordinates q=dz2q=dz^2. Write q(A,B)q(A,B) for the resulting differential. Then Area⁡(q(A,B))=i(A,B),(27.1)gtq(A,B)=q(etA,e−tB),gt=diag⁡(et,e−t).(27.2)\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Area}(q(A,B))&=i(A,B),\\ g_tq(A,B)&=q(e^tA,e^{-t}B), \qquad g_t=\operatorname{diag}(e^t,e^{-t}). \end{aligned} In particular q̂(A,B):=q(Ai(A,B),Bi(A,B))\widehat q(A,B):= q\left(\frac{A}{\sqrt{i(A,B)}}, \frac{B}{\sqrt{i(A,B)}}\right) has unit area. This is a genuine parametrization of points of the flow space; the two foliations are the stable and unstable data of a single differential, rather than two unrelated curves.

For an integral pair A=∑iaiαiA=\sum_i a_i\alpha_i, B=∑jbjβjB=\sum_j b_j\beta_j in minimal position, there is a concrete realization. Put a rectangle of horizontal width aia_i and vertical height bjb_j at each intersection of αi\alpha_i with βj\beta_j, and glue the sides in the cyclic orders supplied by the embedded curves. The filling condition makes the complementary regions disks. Minimal position excludes bigons. A complementary 2k2k-gon yields total angle kπk\pi, hence a zero of order k−2k-2; k=2k=2 gives a regular point. Integral side lengths subdivide the rectangles into unit squares, with edge identifications by translations or half-turns. The total number of squares is ∑i,jaibji(αi,βj)=i(A,B).\sum_{i,j}a_i b_j i(\alpha_i,\beta_j)=i(A,B). Thus the discrete objects suggested by the pair coefficients are precisely square-tiled half-translation surfaces. Translation surfaces form the subcollection in which the half-translation structure has trivial linear holonomy. Formula (27.2) is then visible by stretching and contracting the rectangles.

27.3 The correct quotient and the two different counting scales

Let Γ=Mod⁡(S)\Gamma=\operatorname{Mod}(S). Equivariance of the pair parametrization identifies the stabilizer of the measured pair with the finite automorphism group of its differential: Stab⁡Γ(A,B)=Stab⁡Γ(A)∩Stab⁡Γ(B).\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(A,B) =\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(A)\cap\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(B). For integral pairs, finiteness also follows by acting on the finite filling graph A∪BA\cup B and then on its complementary disks; for general measured foliations it follows from the realized conformal automorphism group. It matters here that one fixes the measured foliations, including their scales. Stabilizers of projective foliations can contain nontrivial flow translations.

One simultaneous Γ\Gamma-orbit of a filling pair represents just one point in moduli space and has constant intersection number. To obtain a growing family, prescribe the two curve types separately and take the diagonal quotient: (Γα×Γβ)fill/Γ.\bigl(\Gamma\alpha\times\Gamma\beta\bigr)_{\mathrm{fill}}/\Gamma. If Γα=Stab⁡Γ(α)\Gamma_\alpha=\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(\alpha) and Γβ=Stab⁡Γ(β)\Gamma_\beta=\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(\beta), its classes are the relevant double cosets in Γα∖Γ/Γβ\Gamma_\alpha\backslash\Gamma/\Gamma_\beta, represented by (α,ϕβ)(\alpha,\phi\beta). For an orbifold volume comparison the natural weight of such a class is 1|Γα∩ϕΓβϕ−1|.\frac{1}{|\Gamma_\alpha\cap \phi\Gamma_\beta\phi^{-1}|}. This is the same stabilizer factor appearing in the square-tiled count of [2, Section 1 and Section 3].

Fix a connected component 𝒬\mathcal Q of a stratum and put h=dim⁡ℂ𝒬h=\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal Q. The real period-coordinate dimension of its cone is 2h2h, and the unit-area locus has real dimension 2h−12h-1. If DrD_r multiplies every period by rr, then area is multiplied by r2r^2. With the appropriate period lattice and its covolume fixed, Lebesgue measure ν\nu scales by ν(DrE)=r2hν(E).\nu(D_r E)=r^{2h}\nu(E). Consequently an area cutoff LL has natural count exponent hh, whereas a common linear scale R=LR=\sqrt L has exponent 2h2h. These conventions must not be interchanged when converting powers into rates in flow time.

For the closed principal quadratic stratum h=6g−6h=6g-6. For a quadratic stratum with nn specified singularities, including marked regular points, h=2g−2+nh=2g-2+n when the differential is not a square; the corresponding square locus has h=2g−1+nh=2g-1+n. Lower-dimensional strata require their own period-coordinate lattice and Masur–Veech measure. The ambient product of two full-dimensional Thurston measures cannot serve as their volume form. Likewise, counting all square-tiled surfaces in a stratum does not by itself approximate an arbitrary affine invariant measure on a smaller locus.

27.4 The existing square-tiled counting theorem and the new target

The passage from pairs to square tilings is established mathematics, not a new consequence of the coefficient construction. Arana-Herrera’s effective theorem counts square-tiled surfaces in a fixed connected component with prescribed horizontal and vertical multicurve types, with a power-saving error in the area cutoff [3, Theorem 1.1]. Its proof explicitly passes through Eskin–Mirzakhani–Mohammadi orbit counting and effective horosphere equidistribution, using exponential Teichmüller mixing [3, Theorem 3.2 and its proof]. Using that estimate to recover mixing would therefore not provide an independent proof of mixing.

There is a useful distinction already visible in that paper. When one foliation type is fixed and the other is unrestricted, standard lattice counting replaces the mapping-class-orbit estimate [3, Theorem 3.9]. This provides a tractable elementary benchmark. It does not control arbitrary initial and terminal observables at long times: their weights must still be tracked through the reciprocal scaling in (27.2).

27.5 What refining the coefficient mesh actually proves

There are two independent limiting operations: approximation of a phase-space integral by rational points, and factorization of that integral under a long flow. The first can be effective without implying the second. Here is a precise local calculation. Put At=diag⁡(etId,e−tId),Ht(x)=F(x)G(Atx),A_t=\operatorname{diag}(e^t I_d,e^{-t}I_d),\qquad H_t(x)=F(x)G(A_tx), where F∈Cc1(ℝ2d)F\in C_c^1(\mathbb R^{2d}) has support in a fixed compact set KK and G∈Cb1(ℝ2d)G\in C_b^1(\mathbb R^{2d}). Then, for t≥0t\geq0, |QR(t)−I(t)|≤CKR−1et∥F∥C1∥G∥C1.|Q_R(t)-I(t)|\leq C_K R^{-1}e^t \|F\|_{C^1}\|G\|_{C^1}.(27.3) Indeed, partition space into cubes of side R−1R^{-1}, compare the value at one corner with the integral on its cube, and use ∥∇Ht∥∞≤∥∇F∥∞∥G∥∞+et∥F∥∞∥∇G∥∞.\|\nabla H_t\|_\infty \leq \|\nabla F\|_\infty\|G\|_\infty +e^t\|F\|_\infty\|\nabla G\|_\infty. Only OK(R2d)O_K(R^{2d}) cubes contribute. A fixed smooth density can be absorbed in FF. To model unit-area data by an ambient lattice, one can first use a smooth radial cutoff in a compact area shell bounded away from area zero; radial normalization is then smooth. This local argument does not supply uniform estimates across arbitrarily many period-coordinate charts, their boundaries, or the cusp.

At the diagonal scale R=etR=e^t, the displayed error has no decay. Taking R=eσtR=e^{\sigma t}, with σ>1\sigma>1, gives a quadrature error O(e−(σ−1)t)O(e^{-(\sigma-1)t}). This removes the resolution obstruction in this particular local model. It does not evaluate the remaining integral I(t)I(t). For Schwartz data, Poisson summation makes the distinction exact: QR(t)=∑n∈ℤ2dĤt(Rn)=I(t)+∑n≠0Ĥt(Rn).Q_R(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^{2d}}\widehat H_t(Rn) =I(t)+\sum_{n\ne0}\widehat H_t(Rn).(27.4) The zero Poisson frequency is the two-time integral itself, not the product of its one-time means. Suppressing the nonzero Poisson frequencies therefore proves accurate quadrature, not mixing. On the quotient, one must still analyze the sum over changes of marking that returns AtKA_tK to a chosen collection of charts.

Even exact sampling identities do not force mixing. On ℝ/ℤ\mathbb R/\mathbb Z, with gt(x)=x+tg_t(x)=x+t and F(x)=G(x)=cos⁡(2πx)F(x)=G(x)=\cos(2\pi x), ∫01F(x)G(gtx)dx=12cos⁡(2πt).\int_0^1F(x)G(g_tx)\,dx=\tfrac12\cos(2\pi t). For every integer R≥3R\geq3, the corresponding equal-mesh average is exactly this integral, by finite Fourier orthogonality. Both means vanish, while the correlation does not decay. This example isolates the logical issue; it is not a model of the full geometry of a stratum.

27.6 An exact weighted bisector kernel

There is a concrete discrete count whose remainder does imply mixing. The necessary weight can be specified exactly, and the passage from a cumulative count to a correlation has an elementary quantitative proof. This construction uses the full unit cotangent bundle, with its Masur–Veech measure supported on the principal stratum; it is not an automatic statement for an arbitrary lower-dimensional stratum or affine invariant measure.

Let SS be closed of genus g≥2g\geq2, put h=6g−6h=6g-6, and replace the mapping class group by a torsion-free finite-index subgroup Γ\Gamma. This removes stabilizer factors. Write Y=Γ∖Q1𝒯(S),B=Γ∖𝒯(S),Y=\Gamma\backslash Q^1\mathcal T(S),\qquad B=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal T(S), and normalize the invariant measure μ\mu on YY to have mass one. Its projection mm to BB also has mass one. Use the same notation for the lifted measures, and disintegrate ⁡dμ(q)=⁡dsx(q)⁡dm(x),q∈S(x),\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\mu(q)=\mathop{}\!\mathrm ds_x(q)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dm(x),\qquad q\in S(x), where S(x)=π−1(x)S(x)=\pi^{-1}(x) and sxs_x is a probability measure. 27.7 Removing the time average with a controlled loss

The next lemma applies to any probability-preserving flow. Write UtG=G∘gtU_tG=G\circ g_t for its strongly continuous unitary action on L2(μ)L^2(\mu), and let VV be the infinitesimal generator, so that ∂tUtG=UtVG\partial_tU_tG=U_tVG on its domain. 27.8 A derivative endpoint weight preserves the full exponent

For a counting estimate uniform over endpoint observables, there is a stronger converse than temporal smoothing. One can differentiate the endpoint test inside the discrete count and use an exact identity, without differentiating an asymptotic formula.

Theorem 27.4 (A counting converse with no exponent loss). Use the notation of Proposition 27.2. Let F∈L2(μ)F\in L^2(\mu) and G∈Dom⁡(V)G\in\operatorname{Dom}(V), and suppose that HG=(h+V)GH_G=(h+V)G is an admissible terminal test for the weighted counting estimate. This optional comparison also has a dependency worth making explicit: the proof of [4, Proposition 2.5 and Section 3.2] uses Forni’s contraction estimate for the Hodge norm of the Kontsevich–Zorich cocycle, quoted there as Theorem 3.3. The exact Jacobian unfolding and the elementary de-averaging theorem above do not use that estimate. Invoking the Hubbard–Masur asymptotic would, however, retain this part of the existing cocycle machinery.

Effective bisector counts are already part of [5, Section 10]; those results are obtained from exponential mixing. They locate the strength of the required counting statement, but do not give an independent proof of mixing when inserted into the converse above. An independent derivation from coefficient distributions or a different counting mechanism would have to establish the required weights and uniformity.

The remaining independent estimate

We have obtained an exact encoding of the filling-pair data, an exact weighted orbit-counting identity, and a quantitative implication from a remainder for that count to mixing. What has not been established is a norm-controlled estimate of the form (27.8) from the coefficient distributions. The tensor product and the first-corner extraction do not by themselves estimate a sum over returning mapping classes. In particular, an exact or quantitative comparison between the resulting square-tiled sums and the bisector kernel has not yet been supplied.

The next analytic target can now be stated without referring vaguely to a good scalar counting error: construct that comparison and control the nonconstant two-endpoint weights in (27.6), with the exact inverse Jacobian or with a proved replacement and a controlled discarded contribution. The modular calculation shows what success looks like. Its scalar count determines the zero mode, while the Kloosterman estimate controls the coupled nonzero modes. No analogous estimate for the principal higher-genus kernel is proved here. Section 28 does close a different, explicitly defined return calculation on the exceptional marked-torus model of the nonsquare genus-two (2,2)(2,2) stratum.

An independent proof of the displayed remainder would yield a new route to mixing for the specified Masur–Veech measure, with the full rate in (27.13) when derivative endpoint weights are controlled, and the cutoff rate (27.17). The single-test estimate (27.9) remains an alternative under its weaker hypothesis. It would not by itself classify invariant measures or orbit closures, nor would it establish spectral properties of a separate cocycle. Those require additional information beyond correlations of the base flow.
