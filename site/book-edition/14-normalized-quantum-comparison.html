14 The normalized quantum-group comparison and the compact limit

The further comparison has two distinct outcomes. The finite screening operator can be matched explicitly to a specified bb-6j6j normalization: its double-Gamma factors reduce to ordinary Gamma factors and its analytic quantum-group matrix is a sine matrix. Here the operator is fixed by the independent period calculation in Section 13; its interpretation through a continued infinite spectral representation retains the analytic input specified there. Its auxiliary angle Gram matrix approaches rank two, whereas a nondegenerate shrinking compact tetrahedron approaches rank three. A direct calculation for the regular compact family gives a cubic classical saddle and fixes the proposed joint scale. The exact integration coordinate requires care: a previously stated pole accumulation at the saddle came from an incorrect shift. The corrected dictionary below and the local pole-free strip identify the candidate quantum fold. Section 15 proves the uniform quantum Airy formula for this pole-cleared regular family and bounded real transition parameters, using the exact-integral contour and tail analysis. The finite screening calculation and the classical fold here do not supply those additional estimates.

14.8 Correction of the integration-coordinate dictionary

An earlier version of this monograph placed a dense pole lattice at the classical fold. That conclusion was incorrect: the logarithmic coordinate of the dilogarithm potential was matched to the wrong shifted integration variable. The exact integral fixes the dictionary without an asymptotic choice of branch.

With all six charges α=(π−η)/(2πb)\alpha=(\pi-\eta)/(2\pi b), write the original integration variable in [96, eq. (2.27)] as ww. Its integrand is Ib(w,η)=Sb(w−3α)4Sb(4α−w)3Sb(2Q−w).I_b(w,\eta)=S_b(w-3\alpha)^4 S_b(4\alpha-w)^3S_b(2Q-w).(14.25) Set a=eiηa=e^{i\eta}. Directly, e2πib(w−3α)=−a3e2πibw.e^{2\pi ib(w-3\alpha)}=-a^3e^{2\pi ibw}. Thus the variable uu in the classical potential is u=e2πibw,v=2πbw.u=e^{2\pi ibw},\qquad v=2\pi bw.(14.26) The Q/2Q/2-shift used previously is incompatible with this identification. A second check is the exact shift quotient Ib(w+b,η)Ib(w,η)=[2sin⁡πb(w−3α)]4[2sin⁡πb(4α−w−b)]3[2sin⁡πb(2Q−w−b)]→(1+a3u)4(1−a4u)3(1−u).(14.27)\begin{aligned} \frac{I_b(w+b,\eta)}{I_b(w,\eta)} &= \frac{[2\sin\pi b(w-3\alpha)]^4} {[2\sin\pi b(4\alpha-w-b)]^3 [2\sin\pi b(2Q-w-b)]}\\ &\longrightarrow \frac{(1+a^3u)^4}{(1-a^4u)^3(1-u)}. \end{aligned} The limit reproduces the saddle polynomial (14.21).

The lift of the double root lying in the separating contour strip is v*=4π−5ηE,w*=v*2πb.v_*=4\pi-5\eta_{\mathrm E},\qquad w_*=\frac{v_*}{2\pi b}. It differs by 2π2\pi from the logarithmic lift used in the classical calculation; all its local derivatives remain the same. At this lift, w*−3αE=π−2ηE2πb,4αE−w*=ηE2πb,2Q−w*=5ηE2πb+2b.\begin{aligned} w_*-3\alpha_{\mathrm E} &=\frac{\pi-2\eta_{\mathrm E}}{2\pi b},\\ 4\alpha_{\mathrm E}-w_*&=\frac{\eta_{\mathrm E}}{2\pi b},\\ 2Q-w_*&=\frac{5\eta_{\mathrm E}}{2\pi b}+2b. \end{aligned}(14.28) All three arguments lie in 0<Re⁡z<Q0<\operatorname{Re}z<Q for sufficiently small bb, a fixed scaled distance from its boundary. There is therefore no integration-pole accumulation at the fold. The external trivalent factors do have parameter poles; their regularization and the further analytic requirements for a uniform transition are treated in the next section. Pole separation in this local strip alone does not control the complete integration contour or its tails.
