A rectangle with two interior vertices

Divide a 3 by 2 rectangle into six unit squares, then divide each square along its northeast diagonal. There are twelve right-isosceles triangles and two interior vertices, numbered 5 and 6. Every face has counterclockwise orientation.

For an oriented triangle (a,b,c), the angle at a changes by ub − uc, with the same rule cyclically. Set u = 0 on the boundary. These leading–trailing angle coordinates preserve each triangle sum and each prescribed opposite-edge angle sum.

The square diagonals have zero cotangent weight. This is a non-strict Delaunay example with flat diagonals, not a certificate of a strictly simplicial convex polyhedron.

Solid grid edges carry the network. Dashed diagonals carry zero weight. Interior vertices are teal.

Eliminate the interior to obtain boundary currents

For this separate electrical problem, prescribe boundary voltages vB. The interior harmonic values and the Dirichlet-to-Neumann matrix are

vI = −LII−1LIBvB,   N = LBB − LBILII−1LIB.

The program uses a linear solve, not an explicitly formed inverse. N is symmetric and annihilates constant boundary voltages. With v = x/3, the interior values are exactly 1/3 and 2/3, and the Dirichlet energy is 2/3.

This control scales the checked linear solution. It is not a new optimization or an angle deformation.

The same elimination in the volume quadratic form

Eliminating the first interior coordinate from K leaves Keff = 8 − (−2)²/8 = 15/2. The determinant factorization is exact: det K = 8 × 15/2 = 60. These angle variables and the electrical boundary voltages are different variables; it is the elimination operation that they share.

The determinant counts rooted forests

A boundary-rooted spanning forest has exactly one boundary vertex in each component. A contributing forest here has two edges. Nine choices attach each interior vertex to a boundary vertex separately. Six choices join the two interior vertices and attach the pair to one of their six boundary neighbors.

∑F rooted at B ∏e ∈ F we = 9 + 6 = 15;   det K = 2² × 15 = 60.

All fifteen nonzero contributions have weight one. Exhaustive enumeration in rational arithmetic agrees with the grounded determinant. This exact calculation concerns this finite fixture; the general identity is the theorem explained in the monograph.
