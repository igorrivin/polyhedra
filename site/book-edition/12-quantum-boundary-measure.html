12 An exact quantum comparison and the boundary measure

The preceding question has a concrete partial answer. A special unit-volume Liouville correlation gives the Deligne–Mostow Kähler potential exactly, at finite quantum parameter. Independently, the boundary Reidemeister-torsion measure reduces to the Goldman measure, whose rescaled flat limit is the Deligne–Mostow measure. Neither statement identifies an arbitrary tetrahedral quantum amplitude with the model integral (11.8). The distinction between a correlation, a Kähler potential, and a volume density is essential.

12.1 A first-moment identity at the flat weights

The calculation has three steps. First a particular charge sum turns the random quantity Z−sZ^{-s} into ZZ, whose expectation is known. Next that expectation is identified with flat area. Finally a mixed derivative of its logarithm produces the geometric two-form. Only the first step is probabilistic; the last two are ordinary integration and differentiation. Section 10.9 gives the zero-mode substitution that explains why s=−1s=-1 is the useful specialization.

Fix the weights (7.1), all marked points finite in the chosen coordinate, and a Liouville parameter 0<b<10<b<1. Set γ=2b,Q=b+b−1,βi=μib,s=∑iβi−2Qγ=−1.\gamma=2b,\qquad Q=b+b^{-1},\qquad \beta_i=\frac{\mu_i}{b},\qquad s=\frac{\sum_i\beta_i-2Q}{\gamma}=-1.(12.1) Here βi\beta_i are the charge conventions of Kupiainen–Rhodes–Vargas; in the physics convention Vαi=e2αiϕV_{\alpha_i}=e^{2\alpha_i\phi}, one has βi=2αi\beta_i=2\alpha_i. Write ΛL>0\Lambda_L>0 for the cosmological constant, to distinguish it from both the deficit weights and the geometric area.

The exact correlation formula of [68, eqs. (2.11)–(2.15)] is Cb(𝜷;𝒑)=1bΛL−sΓ(s)Pb(𝒑)𝔼[Z𝜷−s],(12.2)Pb(𝒑)=∏i<j|pi−pj|−βiβj,Z𝜷=∫ℂ∏i(|w|+|w−pi|)γβiMγ(⁡d2w),|w|+=max⁡(1,|w|),\begin{aligned} C_b(\boldsymbol\beta;\boldsymbol p) &=\frac1b\Lambda_L^{-s}\Gamma(s) P_b(\boldsymbol p)\,\mathbb E[Z_{\boldsymbol\beta}^{-s}], \\ P_b(\boldsymbol p) &=\prod_{i<j}|p_i-p_j|^{-\beta_i\beta_j},\\ Z_{\boldsymbol\beta} &=\int_{\mathbb C}\prod_i \left(\frac{|w|_+}{|w-p_i|}\right)^{\gamma\beta_i} M_\gamma(\mathop{}\!\mathrm d^2w),\qquad |w|_+=\max(1,|w|), \end{aligned} where MγM_\gamma is the normalized Gaussian multiplicative-chaos measure in their reference metric. The only probabilistic property needed for the following calculation is its first moment, 𝔼[Mγ(⁡d2w)]=|w|+−4⁡d2w.\mathbb E[M_\gamma(\mathop{}\!\mathrm d^2w)]=|w|_+^{-4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm d^2w.(12.3) The standard unit-volume normalization removes the zero-mode Gamma factor: Cbuv:=ΛLsΓ(s)Cb=1bPb𝔼[Z𝜷−s].C_b^{\mathrm{uv}} :=\frac{\Lambda_L^s}{\Gamma(s)}C_b =\frac1b P_b\,\mathbb E[Z_{\boldsymbol\beta}^{-s}].(12.4) The right side defines the continuation at s=−1s=-1.

Proof. At (12.1), the exponent −s-s is 11. Moreover, γβi=2μi\gamma\beta_i=2\mu_i and γ∑iβi=4\gamma\sum_i\beta_i=4. Applying (12.3) therefore cancels the reference-metric factors exactly: 𝔼[Z𝜷]=∫ℂ∏i(|w|+|w−pi|)2μi|w|+−4⁡d2w=𝒜μ(𝒑).\mathbb E[Z_{\boldsymbol\beta}] =\int_{\mathbb C} \prod_i\left(\frac{|w|_+}{|w-p_i|}\right)^{2\mu_i} |w|_+^{-4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm d^2w =\mathcal A_\mu(\boldsymbol p). This is a deduction from the established correlation formula and the area interpretation of the period pairing; it does not use the main DOZZ structure-constant theorem. It is also an exact finite-bb identity, rather than an interchange of a semiclassical limit with two moduli derivatives. The raw expression Cbuv⁡d2dpC_b^{\mathrm{uv}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm d^{2d}p is not the moduli-space volume density: the density is the top exterior power of its logarithmic curvature.

12.2 Analytic continuation and the equivalent residue

The original unconstrained zero-mode integral is not convergent at s=−1s=-1. The unit-volume function, however, is positive and finite there. Indeed, the extended Seiberg bounds of [68, eq. (2.14)] require −s<min⁡{4γ2,mini2γ(Q−βi)},βi<Q.-s<\min\left\{\frac4{\gamma^2}, \min_i\frac2\gamma(Q-\beta_i)\right\},\qquad \beta_i<Q. At our parameters these inequalities reduce to 1<b−2,1<1+1−μib2,βi<Q,1<b^{-2},\qquad 1<1+\frac{1-\mu_i}{b^2},\qquad \beta_i<Q,(12.7) and are strict. In particular, the positive moment has some integrability beyond order one. Theorem 6.1 of [68] gives holomorphic continuation in the charges of the unit-volume nn-point correlation to a complex neighborhood of this real domain. Thus the pole in (12.2) is an actual meromorphic pole, not just a formal screening calculation.

Since Res⁡s=−1Γ(s)=−1\operatorname{Res}_{s=-1}\Gamma(s)=-1, Ress=−1Cb=−ΛLbPb𝒜μ.\operatorname*{Res}_{s=-1}C_b =-\frac{\Lambda_L}{b}P_b\mathcal A_\mu.(12.8) This residue means the coefficient of (s+1)−1(s+1)^{-1} with the other charge coordinates fixed. With the transverse physics coordinate t=∑iαi−Q+b=b(s+1)t=\sum_i\alpha_i-Q+b=b(s+1), it becomes Res⁡t=0Cb=−ΛLPb𝒜μ\operatorname{Res}_{t=0}C_b=-\Lambda_LP_b\mathcal A_\mu. Consequently the logarithmic curvature of the absolute residue also equals (12.6).

In Coulomb-gas terminology this is the one-screening resonance ∑iαi=Q−b\sum_i\alpha_i=Q-b; see also [95, Section 3.4]. Its chiral contour integrals are ∫γ∏i(w−pi)−μi⁡dw,\int_\gamma\prod_i(w-p_i)^{-\mu_i}\mathop{}\!\mathrm dw,(12.9) precisely the Deligne–Mostow periods (7.4). Their Hermitian pairing is the area. Section 13 gives an independent finite period calculation of the four-point residue and fusion matrix, and states the additional continuation and tail-control input for the spectral-contour derivation. A pointwise identification with an unnormalized modular-double 6j6j function would still require its normalization and contour to be specified. Section 14 fixes these normalizations for the induced finite operator and examines the geometric meaning of that specialization.

12.3 Four points: the exact quantum potential and its measure

Put the marks at (0,1,z,∞)(0,1,z,\infty), with the standard primary-field normalization at infinity. Then (12.5) reads Cbuv(z)=1b|z|−μ1μ3/b2|1−z|−μ2μ3/b2𝒜μ(z),(12.10)𝒜μ(z)=∫ℂ|w|−2μ1|w−1|−2μ2|w−z|−2μ3⁡d2w.\begin{aligned} C_b^{\mathrm{uv}}(z) &=\frac1b |z|^{-\mu_1\mu_3/b^2} |1-z|^{-\mu_2\mu_3/b^2}\mathcal A_\mu(z), \\ \mathcal A_\mu(z) &=\int_{\mathbb C}|w|^{-2\mu_1}|w-1|^{-2\mu_2} |w-z|^{-2\mu_3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm d^2w. \end{aligned} The condition at infinity is now μ1+μ2+μ3=2−μ4>1\mu_1+\mu_2+\mu_3=2-\mu_4>1. The ordinary field normalization at infinity cancels the corresponding area scaling; there is no additional moduli-dependent factor.

For equal weights μi=1/2\mu_i=1/2, the area is 𝒜(z)=∫ℂ⁡d2w|w(w−1)(w−z)|.\mathcal A(z)=\int_{\mathbb C}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm d^2w}{|w(w-1)(w-z)|}.(12.11) Let Ω1,Ω2\Omega_1,\Omega_2 be oriented lattice periods of ⁡dw/y\mathop{}\!\mathrm dw/y on the elliptic curve (7.9), and put τ=Ω2/Ω1=u+iv∈ℍ\tau=\Omega_2/\Omega_1=u+iv\in\mathbb H. Locally Ω1\Omega_1 is nonzero and holomorphic. Hence −2i∂∂‾log⁡Cbuv=⁡du∧⁡dvv2=|τ′(z)|2(Im⁡τ(z))2⁡d(Re⁡z)∧⁡d(Im⁡z).\boxed{\quad -2i\partial\bar\partial\log C_b^{\mathrm{uv}} =\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm du\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dv}{v^2} =\frac{|\tau'(z)|^2}{(\operatorname{Im}\tau(z))^2} \mathop{}\!\mathrm d(\operatorname{Re}z)\wedge \mathop{}\!\mathrm d(\operatorname{Im}z). \quad}(12.12) The elliptic-integral expression for τ\tau is (7.10). Integrating this form over the labeled moduli space gives 2π2\pi, as in Section 7. This is the flat equal-deficit Deligne–Mostow area; the cusped hyperbolic WP area is the different quantity V0,4(0)=2π2V_{0,4}(0)=2\pi^2.

12.4 An actual boundary torsion measure

Here a boundary conjugacy class fixes the type of each peripheral holonomy; locally a regular SL2\mathrm{SL}_2 conjugacy class is specified by its trace. “Good” means irreducible with only the central stabilizer, and “boundary-regular” means each peripheral centralizer has the minimal dimension. These are the nonsingularity hypotheses of the external torsion theorem. The Goldman form is the symplectic pairing on infinitesimal flat-connection deformations, obtained by intersecting their cohomology classes and pairing the Lie-algebra coefficients with BB.

There is also a measure comparison on the representation side. For a good, boundary-regular SL2(ℂ)\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C) representation of an nn-holed sphere, Heusener–Porti prove ΩS=±ωGdd!∧ν1∧⋯∧νn,d=n−3,\Omega_S=\pm\frac{\omega_G^d}{d!} \wedge\nu_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\nu_n, \qquad d=n-3,(12.13) where ΩS\Omega_S is the holomorphic Reidemeister-torsion volume form, ωG\omega_G is the Goldman form on the fixed-conjugacy leaf, and the νi\nu_i are the peripheral volume forms [61, Theorem 3]. With invariant pairing B(U,V)=−tr⁡(UV)B(U,V)=-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(UV) their peripheral formula is νi=±2⁡dtr⁡ρ(∂i)\nu_i=\pm\sqrt2\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\rho(\partial_i) [61, Proposition 2]. The pairing must be the same in all three kinds of form. For B↦λ2BB\mapsto\lambda^2B, with λ∈ℂ*\lambda\in\mathbb C^*, the homogeneity stated after that theorem is, in our notation, ωG↦λ2ωG,νi↦λνi,ΩS↦λ2d+nΩS.\omega_G\mapsto\lambda^2\omega_G,\qquad \nu_i\mapsto\lambda\nu_i,\qquad \Omega_S\mapsto\lambda^{2d+n}\Omega_S. Thus the displayed 2\sqrt2 belongs specifically to B=−trB=-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}; it is not retained unchanged when the Goldman form is recalibrated. If the scale of BB is negative on the real form, choose its complex square root consistently in the holomorphic forms, then take the positive density on the real leaf.

The elementary model for the next operation is Ω=f(x,y,t)⁡dx∧⁡dy∧⁡dt\Omega=f(x,y,t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dx\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dy\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dt. To obtain the measure at fixed tt, first separate the factor ⁡dt\mathop{}\!\mathrm dt, leaving f(x,y,t)⁡dx∧⁡dyf(x,y,t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dx\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dy, and then set t=t0t=t_0. Pulling the original three-form back to the plane t=t0t=t_0 gives zero because ⁡dt\mathop{}\!\mathrm dt pulls back to zero. The peripheral forms play the role of these transverse factors.

One must disintegrate by the peripheral forms before fixing the conjugacy classes. Simply pulling the unrestricted top-degree form back to a fixed-trace leaf gives zero. After this disintegration, and on the relevant real component, the torsion density is the Goldman Liouville density, with the chosen normalization of BB.

Mondello identifies the cone WP and Goldman Poisson structures up to a specified constant for openings in (0,2π)(0,2\pi) [80, Theorems 1.1–1.2]. We calibrate BB and the torsion and peripheral forms together so that the symplectic form on our real leaf is ωWP\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}, with the positive trace-coordinate density fixed in Appendix A. The holonomy statement is local in general; it is globally injective on marked cone metrics when every opening is at most π\pi. Combining (12.13) with (8.5) gives, locally on a controlled family, (2A)d⁡dVtorsion,relative→⁡dVCH,μ.\boxed{\quad \left(\frac2A\right)^d \mathop{}\!\mathrm dV_{\mathrm{torsion,relative}} \longrightarrow \mathop{}\!\mathrm dV_{\mathrm{CH},\mu}. \quad}(12.14) Apply the torsion theorem for A>0A>0 and then take the rescaled limit. At the Euclidean endpoint the affine holonomies become reducible, and their ordinary characters discard translational shape data. The limiting twisted periods or a blowup retaining their scale are indispensable. In particular, an unqualified ordinary Goldman construction for the Euclidean isometry group would miss the needed nondegenerate pairing.

12.5 The four-point character surface, including a correction

The preceding measure limit can be calculated directly. Give the four cone points the common opening θ=π−A/4\theta=\pi-A/4, so their total hyperbolic area is AA. Choose equal peripheral traces a=−2cos⁡(θ/2)=−2sin⁡(A/8)a=-2\cos(\theta/2)=-2\sin(A/8). The Fricke relation of Appendix A becomes Φ=x2+y2+z2+xyz−2a2(x+y+z)+4a2+a4−4=0.\Phi=x^2+y^2+z^2+xyz-2a^2(x+y+z)+4a^2+a^4-4=0.(12.15) The trace coordinates all approach −2-2, which would erase the differences between limiting shapes. A blowup here simply means retaining their first scaled deviations from −2-2. It is an explicit change of coordinates, not an assertion that the unscaled limiting character still distinguishes the shapes.

Blow up the limiting character by x=−2−AX,y=−2−AY,z=−2−AZ.x=-2-AX,\qquad y=-2-AY,\qquad z=-2-AZ.(12.16) Direct substitution gives ΦA2=X2+Y2+Z2−2XY−2XZ−2YZ+1+A(−XYZ+X+Y+Z8)−A2768+O(A3).(12.17)\begin{aligned} \frac\Phi{A^2} ={}&X^2+Y^2+Z^2-2XY-2XZ-2YZ+1\\ &+A\left(-XYZ+\frac{X+Y+Z}{8}\right) -\frac{A^2}{768}+O(A^3). \end{aligned} On a chart with u>0u>0, v>0v>0, parametrize the limiting quadric by X=12v,Y=u2+v22v,Z0=(u−1)2+v22v.X=\frac1{2v},\qquad Y=\frac{u^2+v^2}{2v},\qquad Z_0=\frac{(u-1)^2+v^2}{2v}.(12.18) These are squared side lengths of a Euclidean triangle of area 1/41/4; the quadric equation is Heron’s identity. Since X+Y−Z0=uv,⁡dX∧⁡dY=u2v3⁡du∧⁡dv,X+Y-Z_0=\frac uv,\qquad \mathop{}\!\mathrm dX\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dY=\frac{u}{2v^3}\mathop{}\!\mathrm du\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dv, the calibrated density (A.2) yields A−1ωWP→12⁡du∧⁡dv/v2A^{-1}\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}\to\frac12\mathop{}\!\mathrm du\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dv/v^2, with the positive orientation on this chart.

In fact, the implicit-function expansion is Z=Z0+AZ1+O(A2),Z1=(1−u)[v2−u(1−u)]16v2.Z=Z_0+AZ_1+O(A^2),\qquad Z_1=\frac{(1-u)[v^2-u(1-u)]}{16v^2}. This is an explicit first correction in a fixed blown-up character chart. Here τ=u+iv\tau=u+iv is the limiting flat period coordinate, not the exact period ratio or conformal cross-ratio of the finite-AA surface. The error is local on compact subsets of the chart. In particular, the displayed correction is not termwise integrable at v=∞v=\infty; it cannot be integrated naively to recover the global polynomial of (8.10).

12.6 What a normalized quantum kernel would have to produce

The phase-space measure of a unitary change of polarization is more constrained than a bare scalar 6j6j prefactor. In one degree of freedom, let S(q,Q)S(q,Q) generate a canonical relation, p=∂qSp=\partial_qS, away from its caustic, meaning where the projection used for these coordinates degenerates. In this chart the required nondegeneracy is ∂q∂QS≠0\partial_q\partial_QS\ne0. A branchwise normalized semiclassical kernel has the leading form Kℏ(q,Q)∼|∂q∂QS|1/22πℏexp⁡(iSℏ+iπm4).K_\hbar(q,Q)\sim \frac{|\partial_q\partial_QS|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}} \exp\left(\frac{iS}{\hbar}+\frac{i\pi m}{4}\right).(12.20) Here mm is an integer recording the contour or Gaussian phase convention on the chosen branch; it is unrelated to spin labels in the earlier physics box. For S(q,Q)=qQS(q,Q)=qQ, the mixed derivative is one, and the kernel is the Fourier kernel (2πℏ)−1/2eiqQ/ℏ(2\pi\hbar)^{-1/2}e^{iqQ/\hbar} up to this phase. Its squared modulus times 2πℏ⁡dq⁡dQ2\pi\hbar\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm dq\mathop{}\!\mathrm dQ is just ⁡dq⁡dQ\mathop{}\!\mathrm dq\mathop{}\!\mathrm dQ, illustrating the measure identity. The amplitude follows by applying unitarity to the leading phase difference (q−q′)p(q-q')p and changing variables from QQ to pp. Thus 2πℏ|Kℏ|2⁡dq⁡dQ∼|∂q∂QS|⁡dq⁡dQ=|⁡dq∧⁡dp|.2\pi\hbar|K_\hbar|^2\mathop{}\!\mathrm dq\mathop{}\!\mathrm dQ \sim |\partial_q\partial_QS|\mathop{}\!\mathrm dq\mathop{}\!\mathrm dQ =|\mathop{}\!\mathrm dq\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dp|.(12.21) This is a branchwise assertion. Several stationary branches produce interference; a pointwise square of an oscillatory cosine need not converge without averaging.

Teschner–Vartanov’s Fenchel–Nielsen transition kernel contains the factors M(αs)M(αt)\sqrt{M(\alpha_s)M(\alpha_t)} multiplying the bb-6j6j symbol [96, eq. (5.14)]. Consequently a proposed measure comparison must include the spectral measure, label Jacobians, and polarization. If a normalized kernel quantizes our cone WP leaf, its classical measure is constrained by (12.21), and its rescaled flat measure by (12.14). This conditional argument does not by itself establish the elliptic continuation of that kernel.

12.7 The auxiliary tetrahedron and the order of limits

There is a concrete reason not to identify the auxiliary tetrahedron in a fusion kernel with the convex realization of our cone sphere without further work. In the conventions of [96, Sections 3–5], the parameters satisfy αk=Q2+iℓk4πb,Ak=e−2πibαk+πi=eℓk/2−πib2.\alpha_k=\frac Q2+\frac{i\ell_k}{4\pi b},\qquad A_k=e^{-2\pi ib\alpha_k+\pi i} =e^{\ell_k/2-\pi ib^2}.(12.22) Here AkA_k is the paper’s exponentiated tetrahedral parameter, not our geometric area AA. Continuing a boundary length by ℓi=iθi\ell_i=i\theta_i gives an auxiliary dihedral parameter ηi=θi/2−πb2\eta_i=\theta_i/2-\pi b^2, modulo the chosen branch. Thus at the equal-angle Euclidean endpoint, and as b→0b\to0, the four external auxiliary dihedrals tend to π/2\pi/2. The internal s,ts,t edges are opposite. After reordering faces, the limiting angle Gram matrix would be G=(1−cos⁡ηs00−cos⁡ηs100001−cos⁡ηt00−cos⁡ηt1),det⁡G=sin⁡2ηssin⁡2ηt.G=\begin{pmatrix} 1&-\cos\eta_s&0&0\\ -\cos\eta_s&1&0&0\\ 0&0&1&-\cos\eta_t\\ 0&0&-\cos\eta_t&1 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det G=\sin^2\eta_s\sin^2\eta_t.(12.23) For interior angles ηs,ηt∈(0,π)\eta_s,\eta_t\in(0,\pi) this is nonsingular. A nondegenerate Euclidean tetrahedron has a rank-three angle Gram matrix. Hence this real slice cannot converge to the generic Euclidean tetrahedron realizing our flat cone metric. An additional degeneration, a different real slice, or a different geometric identification is needed. This is an obstruction to the proposed identification, not to the existence of Liouville theory with cone charges.

There is also a finite-bb shift in the charge convention. At a fixed classical opening θi=2π(1−μi)\theta_i=2\pi(1-\mu_i), the exact dictionary (12.22) gives αi=μi2b+b2,\alpha_i=\frac{\mu_i}{2b}+\frac b2, whereas (12.1) uses αi=μi/(2b)\alpha_i=\mu_i/(2b). The latter corresponds in that dictionary to the shifted opening θi+2πb2\theta_i+2\pi b^2. For unshifted flat openings the zero-mode parameter would be s=n/2−1s=n/2-1, not −1-1. Thus the exact correlation identity is a specified charge specialization; it is not an identification with the finite-bb kernel at unshifted classical cone angles. Indeed, treating the shifted openings as hyperbolic cone data would give the negative Gauss–Bonnet area −2πnb2-2\pi n b^2.

There is a second, independent distinction. Along a heavy-charge path with deficit weights ai(A)a_i(A), set αi(A)=ai(A)/(2b)\alpha_i(A)=a_i(A)/(2b) and A=2π(∑iai(A)−2)A=2\pi(\sum_i a_i(A)-2). Then exactly s=A4πb2−1.\boxed{\quad s=\frac{A}{4\pi b^2}-1.\quad}(12.24) The finite-bb flat endpoint of this pure-heavy charge path is s=−1s=-1, the resonance used above. The original zero-mode convergence region s>0s>0 requires A>4πb2A>4\pi b^2. In contrast, under the proposed joint scaling A≍ℏ2/3A\asymp\hbar^{2/3} with ℏ≍b2\hbar\asymp b^2, one has A/b2→∞A/b^2\to\infty and hence s→+∞s\to+\infty. The exact residue identity therefore establishes a quantum origin for the Deligne–Mostow curvature without proving the uniform quantum degeneration proposed in Section 11.
