5 Boundary lengths, cone angles, and continuation

5.1 Fixed signature and the polynomial chamber

Allow aa cone points with opening angles θ1,…,θa\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_a and bb geodesic boundary components with lengths L1,…,LbL_1,\ldots,L_b, including zero lengths for cusps. Thus n=a+bn=a+b and d=3g−3+nd=3g-3+n. The area is Area⁡(X)=2π(2g−2+n)−∑i=1aθi>0.\operatorname{Area}(X)=2\pi(2g-2+n)-\sum_{i=1}^{a}\theta_i>0.(5.1) There is no separate puncture parameter in the volume polynomial: a cusp is represented by L=0L=0, or equivalently by cone opening zero.

Use the logarithmic tautological class κ1=π*(ψn+12)\kappa_1=\pi_*(\psi_{n+1}^2) on ℳ¯g,n\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}. The fixed-boundary WP class, continued to cone parameters, is Ω0=2π2κ1+12∑j=1bLj2ψj−12∑i=1aθi2ψi.\Omega_0=2\pi^2\kappa_1+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{b}L_j^2\psi_j -\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{a}\theta_i^2\psi_i.(5.2) The labels in the two sums are understood to be disjoint. In its geometric validity chamber, Vg,n(𝑳,i𝜽)=1d!∫ℳ¯g,nΩ0d.V_{g,n}(\mathbf L,i\boldsymbol\theta)=\frac1{d!} \int_{\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}}\Omega_0^d.(5.3) The boundary formula follows from the symplectic-reduction/intersection description of WP volumes [76]; imaginary lengths L=iθL=i\theta account for the minus sign [55].

For mixed positive boundaries and cone points, a safe geometric regime is 0≤θi<π0\le\theta_i<\pi. For pure cone/cusp surfaces, the uncorrected formula holds in the larger chamber where every pair of openings sums to less than 2π2\pi [45, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2]. In both statements the area condition (5.1) and the prescribed labels remain part of the hypotheses. Polynomial evaluation outside these ranges is always algebraically defined, but requires a separate geometric justification. In particular, a single cone of arbitrary opening below 2π2\pi, with only cusps as the other ends, does not create a cone-collision wall.

For example, on ℳ¯0,4\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,4} one has ∫κ1=∫ψi=1\int\kappa_1=\int\psi_i=1, so V0,4(𝑳,i𝜽)=2π2+12∑Lj2−12∑θi2V_{0,4}(\mathbf L,i\boldsymbol\theta) =2\pi^2+\frac12\sum L_j^2-\frac12\sum\theta_i^2(5.4) whenever this chamber formula applies. On ℳ¯1,1\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,1}, ∫κ1=∫ψ1=1/24\int\kappa_1=\int\psi_1=1/24, giving the torus normalization in (1.2) and its one-cone continuation.

5.2 Pure cone/cusp volumes beyond the first chamber

For 0≤θi<2π0\le\theta_i<2\pi, put wi=1−θi2π,ΦS=∑i∈Sθi−2π(|S|−1),u+=max⁡(u,0).w_i=1-\frac{\theta_i}{2\pi},\qquad \Phi_S=\sum_{i\in S}\theta_i-2\pi(|S|-1),\qquad u_+=\max(u,0). Assume 2g−2+∑iwi>02g-2+\sum_iw_i>0. A collision of the marked points in SS is allowed in the weighted compactification when ∑i∈Swi≤1\sum_{i\in S}w_i\le1. The class pulled back to ordinary Deligne–Mumford space is Ω𝜽=2π2κ1−12∑iθi2ψi+12∑S(ΦS)+2δ0:S.\boxed{ \Omega_{\boldsymbol\theta}=2\pi^2\kappa_1-\frac12\sum_i\theta_i^2\psi_i +\frac12\sum_S(\Phi_S)_+^2\,\delta_{0:S}.}(5.5) The sum runs over stable rational-tail divisors, with |S|≥2|S|\ge2. The geometric volume is ∫Ω𝜽d/d!\int\Omega_{\boldsymbol\theta}^{d}/d!. This is the wall-crossing class of Anagnostou–Mullane–Norbury [44, Definitions 2.12–2.13 and Lemma 2.14], also expressed in the cone-volume formulation of Eberhardt–Turiaci [56]. Here stability means that both components of the nodal curve are stable in ordinary DM space; in genus zero this requires also |Sc|≥2|S^c|\ge2. All classes in (5.5), including κ1\kappa_1 and ψi\psi_i, are on that ordinary compactification. They must not be replaced by weighted-space classes without applying the reduction pullback.

For completeness, the coefficient follows immediately from the weighted formula: its normalized correction is (1−∑i∈Swi)2δ0:S(1-\sum_{i\in S}w_i)^2\delta_{0:S}, and multiplication by 2π22\pi^2 gives (ΦS)2/2(\Phi_S)^2/2. If rr is the reduction to the relevant weighted compactification, then rr is proper and birational. Therefore r*[ℳ¯g,n]=[ℳ¯g,𝒘]r_*[\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}]=[\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,\mathbf w}], and the projection formula justifies integrating the top power of the pulled-back class on ordinary DM space. On a wall ΦS=0\Phi_S=0 the added term vanishes. The formula therefore extends continuously from adjacent chambers, although its polynomial expression changes across the wall. In dimension greater than one the top power includes products and self-intersections of the boundary divisors; adding their coefficients as scalar volume corrections would not compute the volume.

In genus zero δ0:S=δ0:Sc\delta_{0:S}=\delta_{0:S^c}. There is no positive-term double counting in (5.5): stability gives ∑iwi>2\sum_iw_i>2, so both SS and its complement cannot have weight at most one.

For four labeled cone/cusp points the formula becomes particularly simple: V0,4(i𝜽)=2π2−12∑i=14θi2+12∑1≤i<j≤4(θi+θj−2π)+2.\boxed{ V_{0,4}(i\boldsymbol\theta)=2\pi^2-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^4\theta_i^2 +\frac12\sum_{1\le i<j\le4}(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\pi)_+^2.}(5.6) For openings (0,0,3π/2,3π/2)(0,0,3\pi/2,3\pi/2), the area is positive but the uncorrected polynomial gives −π2/4-\pi^2/4. The single active correction contributes π2/2\pi^2/2, giving the actual positive volume π2/4\pi^2/4. This example pinpoints the failure of unrestricted polynomial continuation.

With a positive geodesic boundary and a cone opening greater than π\pi, additional degenerations can occur when the cone approaches that boundary. Anagnostou–Norbury exhibit such a nonnodal degeneration when the positive boundary length is sufficiently large [45, Lemma 2.10]. We consequently assert the full wall formula here for pure cones/cusps, and the preceding small-cone regime for arbitrary mixed positive boundaries. An unrestricted mixed-signature geometric theorem would require its own hypotheses and boundary analysis.

5.3 What continuation means for compact polyhedra

The boundary of a compact convex hyperbolic polyhedron has intrinsic cone opening θv\theta_v equal to the sum of its face angles at vv; its singular curvature is 2π−θv2\pi-\theta_v. Neither is an individual dihedral angle. Thus the substitution L↦iθL\mapsto i\theta concerns an intrinsic holonomy parameter. It is not a literal substitution of arbitrary hyperideal vertex coordinates by compact ones.

At a local algebraic level the continuation is natural. For example, KL(s)=sinh⁡(L/2−s)sinh⁡(L/2)↦sin⁡(θ/2−α)sin⁡(θ/2)under (L,s)=(iθ,iα).K_L(s)=\frac{\sinh(L/2-s)}{\sinh(L/2)} \quad\longmapsto\quad \frac{\sin(\theta/2-\alpha)}{\sin(\theta/2)} \quad\text{under }(L,s)=(i\theta,i\alpha).(5.7) Trace and Gram formulas have analogous analytic continuations. Recovering a real geometric volume also requires a real geometric domain, a valid coordinate chart, and the appropriate collision chamber. The polynomial example above shows why these cannot be inferred from the formal algebra.
