A A trace-coordinate check of the WP factor

A separate two-dimensional calculation is useful for checking the factor 22 in the local densities. Let A,B,C,D∈SL2(ℝ)A,B,C,D\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R) satisfy ABCD=IABCD=I, and set a=tr⁡A,b=tr⁡B,c=tr⁡C,d=tr⁡D,x=tr⁡(AB),y=tr⁡(AC),z=tr⁡(BC).a=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A,\ b=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}B,\ c=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}C,\ d=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}D, \qquad x=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AB),\ y=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AC),\ z=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(BC). With the four peripheral traces fixed, the character surface satisfies the Fricke relation Φ(x,y,z)=x2+y2+z2+xyz−(ab+cd)x−(ac+bd)y−(ad+bc)z+a2+b2+c2+d2+abcd−4=0.(A.1)\begin{aligned} \Phi(x,y,z)={}&x^2+y^2+z^2+xyz-(ab+cd)x-(ac+bd)y\\ &-(ad+bc)z+a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2+abcd-4=0. \end{aligned} This is the standard four-holed-sphere trace surface; see [59].

On a chart where x=2cosh⁡(ℓ/2)>2x=2\cosh(\ell/2)>2, put s=sinh⁡(ℓ/2)s=\sinh(\ell/2) and X=ABX=AB. Twisting across the ABAB cuff fixes A,BA,B and conjugates C,DC,D by exp⁡(τ2HX),HX=X−(x/2)Is.\exp\left(\frac{\tau}{2}H_X\right), \qquad H_X=\frac{X-(x/2)I}{s}. The eigenvalues of HXH_X are 1,−11,-1, so this is a displacement of length τ\tau along the cuff axis. Differentiating y=tr⁡(AC)y=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AC) gives ∂y∂τ=12str⁡(A[X,C])=−Φz2s.\frac{\partial y}{\partial\tau} =\frac1{2s}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\bigl(A[X,C]\bigr) =-\frac{\Phi_z}{2s}. For the last equality, Cayley–Hamilton gives tr⁡(A2BC)=ad−z\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^2BC)=ad-z, while the two-matrix trace identity gives tr⁡(ACAB)=xy−bc+z\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(ACAB)=xy-bc+z. Their difference is ad+bc−xy−2z=−Φzad+bc-xy-2z=-\Phi_z.

Since ⁡dx=s⁡dℓ\mathop{}\!\mathrm dx=s\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\ell and ωWP=⁡dℓ∧⁡dτ\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}=\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\ell\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\tau, ⁡dVWP=2|⁡dx∧⁡dy||Φz|.\boxed{\mathop{}\!\mathrm dV_{\mathrm{WP}}=\frac{2|\mathop{}\!\mathrm dx\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dy|}{|\Phi_z|}.}(A.2) Equivalently, on this twist chart, ⁡dx∧⁡dy=−Φz⁡dℓ∧⁡dτ/2\mathop{}\!\mathrm dx\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm dy=-\Phi_z\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\ell\wedge\mathop{}\!\mathrm d\tau/2. Reversing the chosen twist direction reverses the signed form but leaves the displayed positive density unchanged. The hyperbolic application requires the smooth geometric component of the relative character surface and Φz≠0\Phi_z\ne0 for (x,y)(x,y) to be coordinates. If Φz=0\Phi_z=0 but ∇Φ≠0\nabla\Phi\ne0, another pair can be chosen; if all three partial derivatives vanish, the character surface is singular and this inversion argument does not supply a chart. In particular, arbitrary real solutions of Φ=0\Phi=0 are not automatically holonomies of four-holed spheres. The formula is local on the marked character surface and does not describe a global mapping-class fundamental domain.
