## Section 15: reviewed regular-family theorem and exact-integrand numerics

The [Airy chapter](chapters/15-quantum-airy.tex), `sec:quantumAiry`, now has an independent analytic review of its specified regular-family transition for fixed bounded real zeta. The proof explicitly fixes the convergent double-Gamma/double-sine normalization, including the factor-two subtraction typo in TV v3 (A.1); establishes the phase constant through the real Murakami–Yano formula; relocates arbitrary separating contours at fixed b; and proves uniform global tails, a displaced Gaussian bound, the integrated Taylor remainder and the oriented first correction. The [claim ledger](claims/section15.json) records these analytic dependencies separately from finite computations. The theorem does not cover arbitrary tetrahedral shapes, unbounded zeta, fixed nonzero volume or a global quantum invariant.

The [new companion](../experiments/section15_quantum/README.md) evaluates the exact cleared Barnes integrand using the centered identity for log S_b. A finite cotangent-derivative expansion of D_b has an exact rational truncation bound on the declared centered strip. The five logarithms are combined before exponentiation; the Airy approximation is computed only for comparison. The seven inputs are the six printed pairs and `b=1/160,zeta=-1`. Each has 60-digit `C=1,T=12`, 90-digit `C=1,T=14`, and 90-digit `C=5/4,T=14` evaluations, with two extra D terms in the last setting and separate shorter-cutoff extensions. Direct D and full-log-S integrals, reflection and shift identities, and an independent combined-Barnes QUADPACK evaluation provide additional finite checks.

The source-recorded 21-quadrature matrix has observed 60/90-digit differences below `4e-57` and contour/order differences below `1.3e-91`; the separate whole-Barnes reference agrees on all seven inputs within `2e-12`. These are measured agreements, not certified achieved digits. The exact rational bound controls D-series truncation conditional on the centered-strip hypothesis, not floating-point evaluation. Propagated correction errors use numerical absolute-integral estimates; outer quadrature errors, sign checks and tail-majorant formulas lack outward rounding. No total interval quadrature enclosure is claimed.

The new first quantum value is `0.459086488736078952...`, which rounds to `0.459086` rather than the supplied `0.459087`; the other five rounded values agree. The three new zeta=1 scaled residuals are approximately `1.208081684`, `1.211366819`, and `1.208878763`. The negative-parameter check gives `Q_(1/160)(-1)=0.517966671856531...`. The original evaluator, raw values and rounding convention remain unrecovered. The [historical JSON](../experiments/section15/historical-table.json) is unchanged; new evidence does not reconstruct its missing provenance.
