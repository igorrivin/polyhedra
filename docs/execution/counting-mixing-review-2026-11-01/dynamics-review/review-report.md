## Independently derived checks with particular diagnostic value

1. **Jordan loss is real.** Let `u=(1,−1,0)`, `v=(1,1,−2)`, `N=uvᵀ`, and `Π=11ᵀ/3`. Then `N²=ΠN=NΠ=0`, and
   `A=10Π+(I−Π)+N=[[5,4,1],[2,3,5],[3,3,4]]` is a positive integer adjacency matrix. Thus `tr(A^n)=10^n+2`, while for `P=A/10`,
   `P^n−Π=10^{−n}[(I−Π)+nN]`.
   This proves directly why traces do not remove the polynomial operator loss. The script verifies the exact rational identities for n=1,…,40; the displayed nilpotent algebra proves them for all n.

2. **The genus-two infinity cases close the classification.** With `w=1/z` in an even-degree sextic model, `P_d(z) dz²/y²` has order `2−d` at each of the two infinities. Degree zero gives the square case with two double zeros; degree one gives two simple zeros at infinity; degree two gives no infinity zero. A double finite root is again square, while two distinct branch roots give precisely the required nonsquare double zeros. This avoids silently discarding polynomial degrees zero and one.

3. **The two independent time conventions are consistent.** Section 26's compact hyperbolic geodesic flow uses `diag(e^{t/2},e^{−t/2})`; Sections 27–29 use the Teichmüller/affine `diag(e^t,e^{−t})`. In the latter, the spherical majorant decays as `(1+t)e^{−t}`, the two-mark unstable exponent is `2+1+1=4`, and the quasiconformal length derivative bound has factor 2.

4. **The rank-two Haar constant can be derived without trusting the final formula.** In the coordinates above, `tr(gᵀg)=r²+r^{−2}+s²r²`. Integrating in s, then using `dx=2πr dr` and
   `∫₀∞exp[−a(r²+r^{−2})]dr=√π e^{−2a}/(2√a)`, gives
   `∫_G exp[−αk tr(gᵀg)] dg=π²e^{−2αk}/(αkζ(2))`.
   The Laplace transform, the two determinant signs, and the determinant-orbit multiplicity then reproduce both the Schwartz bound and Gaussian covariance.

5. **Cusp balancing does not hide a derivative cost.** For the smoothed converse the error exponents are `δ−by−x`, `kx−dy`, and `ay`, with `H=e^{yt}` and `ε=e^{−xt}`. Equating them gives `η=δ/[1+b/a+(1+d/a)/k]`. In the terminal-derivative converse there is no smoothing parameter, but b must include the derivative cutoff norm. This is why the full-rate cusp formula has the stated, stronger hypotheses.
