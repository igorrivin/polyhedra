# Section 10.1: physics, Regge calculus, and the purpose of the state sum

This reader-driven revision supplies the missing steps between recoupling algebra and quantum geometric invariants. It starts from the published r3 edition at `249e21888106700170a8bd4caf2b52b20df36d59`.

The physics box explains Pauli matrices, the spin label, addition of angular momenta, singlet/triplet states, and Wigner/Racah recoupling. The existing two-dimensional example now has a measurement interpretation with probabilities 1/4 and 3/4. The box then previews the separate connection to three-dimensional Euclidean gravity.

The geometric development begins with Regge's piecewise Euclidean scheme. Transverse sectors explain the deficit angle, and five regular tetrahedra give a concrete nonzero deficit. The three-dimensional action includes its boundary term; the Euclidean Schläfli identity gives the zero-deficit stationary equations at fixed boundary lengths. Interior and exterior dihedral angles are distinguished before the Ponzano–Regge formula. The large-spin formula states its nondegenerate Euclidean hypothesis, shifted lengths and symmetric Wigner normalization. An equal-spin table compares exact rational Racah values with the leading oscillatory term.

The state-sum discussion explains multiplication of local amplitudes, summation of unobserved internal data, dimension factors and the divergence problem of the undeformed model. Turaev–Viro changes labels and weights compatibly. A triangular bipyramid gives the two-versus-three-tetrahedra example behind the pentagon identity. The finite sum is introduced only after this motivation, followed by a distinction between Euclidean, spherical and hyperbolic asymptotic regimes.
